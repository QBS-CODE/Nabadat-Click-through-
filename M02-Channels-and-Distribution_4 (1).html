How the numbers reconcile. Rules matched 27,070 transactions in the last 7 days. 24,220 survived the dispatch guardrails and were handed to a provider — that is the Messages dispatched tile, and the sum of the Sent · 7 d column. The remaining 2,850 were held or dropped at dispatch. Of what was dispatched, 96.8% came back with a delivery confirmation. Dispatched is not delivered, and matched is not dispatched.

Delivery confirmation is not available on every channel. SMS is confirmed by polling the A2A status service; Email is confirmed by provider events. Where a tenant gateway reports no status at all, a dispatch ends its life unconfirmed rather than delivered — not a failure, and excluded from the delivery-rate denominator rather than counted as zero.
A channel must be enabled here before a trigger rule can select it. Disabling a channel does not delete rules that use it — those rules fall back to their fallback channel, and are flagged on the rules list if no fallback is set. Contact validity, opt-out state and preferred-channel are owned by M-03; Nabadat reads them at dispatch.

Dispatch guardrails

A trigger rule makes a decision. Sending is a separate, later act — dispatch — and these three protections are enforced there, not inside any rule. Each one states which module owns it and why it cannot live in a rule's conditions.
Decision, then dispatch
A trigger rule answers one question at one moment: given this transaction, should a survey be created, which one, and through which channel. Delivering the message is a separate, later act. These three protections deliberately live in the later act.

Fatigue caps M-03 Read-only in M-02
"No more than twice in 30 days" needs memory of the customer across all rules, all surveys and all channels. A trigger rule sees one transaction in isolation and has no such memory, so only a customer-centric module can enforce a cross-cutting cap. M-02 sends a dispatch intent; M-03 answers allow or deny.

Opt-out and consent M-03 Never overridable

Rule exemptions
Which guardrails an individual trigger rule may ask to bypass, and who actually grants it. M-02 can only waive what M-02 enforces; the rest is a request passed to the owning module at dispatch.
Quiet hours M-02 Waivable in M-02
M-02 owns the window, so it can waive it — for an incident survey that has to go out tonight. Requires the rules.override_quiet_hours permission, and every waived send is tagged on the request record.
Fatigue caps M-03 Request only
The rule's flag is forwarded to M-03 as an exemption request at dispatch. M-03 holds the customer history and makes the call — M-02 records what it asked for and what it was told.
Grace period M-03 Request only
Same mechanism. Typically used by complaint follow-up, where a second contact inside the window is the entire point.

Survey trigger rules

Decide whether a survey is sent for a transaction, which survey, in which language, and through which sending channel. Rules read M-13's transaction parameters and channel contracts; the do-not-send policy can still stop the send afterwards.

A rule matching does not guarantee a send. A rule makes the decision; the guardrails run later, at dispatch — quiet hours in M-02, opt-out and fatigue caps in M-03 — so Matched and Sent can legitimately differ, and the gap is not a defect. Hover a gap to see which stage stopped the difference, or open the survey request log for the per-transaction trace. Drag the ⠿ grip to reorder priority within a service channel (first match wins). Rule evaluation is invoked by M-13's transaction pipeline; each evaluation writes a per-rule trace against the transaction's correlation ID.
